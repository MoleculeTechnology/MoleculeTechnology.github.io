Enflux / Motion capture

Movement as an interface

Applications and tools connecting motion capture clothing to real-time experiences and animation workflows.

Contribution
Unity SDK, applications, live demonstrations, and Blender capture/export tooling
Context
Motion capture clothing, developer tools, and public demonstrations

A moving person as the input #

At Enflux, I built software that connected motion capture clothing to interactive applications and creative workflows. The work ranged from a Unity SDK for developers to first-party applications and live demonstrations.

The interesting part was the connection: turning the movement of a person into something that an application, artist, or participant could use.

Tools for developers and artists #

I shipped the Unity SDK used by business partners and consumer developers to work with the motion capture clothing. I also developed a Blender plugin to record motion captured animation and export FBX files.

A companion Unity-based Windows application sent smooth motion data over Wi-Fi to Blender on other computers. This connected the real-time capture experience with an animation workflow beyond the demo itself.

Bringing it in front of people #

My work included first-party applications, investor demonstrations, workshops, tech talks, and hackathons. I also managed trade show booths for CES, SVVR, and SIGGRAPH.

Demonstrating an interactive application in person makes the relationship between input and response especially important. The software has to communicate what it does through the experience itself.

My contribution #

My responsibilities covered software, SDKs, applications, and creative tooling, working alongside the team developing the motion capture clothing.

That experience informs my ongoing light and motion work as Molecule.
