# Brief and positioning

## The desired change

Move from a website introducing a software framework to a portfolio that makes Russell's creative work, authorship, and engineering depth immediately apparent.
The intended outcome is more opportunities to build creative art and technology installations, especially interactive lighting.
Senior engineering work remains a valuable, visible part of the offer.

The working audience priority is installation commissions and creative technology collaborations first, with graphics and tools engineering opportunities also served.
This follows Russell's request and can be adjusted after the audience question is answered.

## Positioning

**Primary description:** Software engineer & artist.

**Supporting description:** Interactive light, motion, and real-time graphics.

**Professional search language:** Creative technologist, graphics engineer, rendering, content pipelines, embedded software, interaction design, mobile and multi-platform development.
Use those terms naturally in project descriptions and metadata.
Do not turn the hero into a list of job titles.

The distinct proposition is the ability to connect visual intent, real-time software, artist tools, sensing, and physical output.
Show how that connection works in specific projects.
The existing experience is stronger than an aspirational statement about wanting to make installations.

## Why these projects belong together

- **Paper Ghost:** Software and visuals worked within real sets, projection equipment, actor interactions, creative direction, and production deadlines.
- **Enflux:** Movement became something software could interpret, visualize, record, and use in live demonstrations.
- **Blizzard:** Core engine work, content delivery, artist tooling, and platform constraints supported creative games for broad audiences.
- **Molecule:** Independent light and sensor projects make the current artistic direction tangible.
- **Moongoat:** A completed pop-up, reported by Russell, provides an important real-world chapter once its media and particulars are assembled.
- **InContext:** The historical CV describes engineering and product design for a multiplayer VR installation, adding another relevant physical-experience story once its final form is confirmed.

These are connected parts of a practice, not unrelated careers.
The site should let a visitor move from an experience to the technical decisions that enabled it.

## Audience needs

| Audience                           | What they need to understand                                               | Evidence to show                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Artist, curator, or commissioner   | What does the work feel like, and what is Russell's artistic contribution? | Strong footage, intent, audience interaction, authorship             |
| Creative studio or production team | Can Russell collaborate and deliver under physical constraints?            | Paper Ghost, Enflux demos, setup notes, collaborator credits         |
| Technical lead or hiring manager   | How deep is the engineering experience?                                    | Blizzard, rendering studies, artist tools, platform work             |
| Venue or event producer            | What would bringing an experience into a space involve?                    | Pop-up documentation, footprint, operation, setup and recovery notes |

Do not promise every fabrication, electrical, venue, or production service as an existing capability.
For each project, say what Russell handled and what collaborators supplied.

## Brand relationship

Use **MOLECULE** as the primary mark and **Russell Jahn — software engineer & artist** beside or directly beneath it.
The proposed description is “Molecule is the creative practice of Russell Jahn.”
That wording is a recommendation, not a claim about a registered business or a staffed studio.

Use first-person singular for authored work.
Credit collaborators by name and role.
Keep the domain's playful “I love Molecule” character without making the navigation or contact language cryptic.
The existing character/logo can provide personality in secondary placements after its current preferred version is identified.

## What the portfolio should demonstrate

1. **Taste:** Composition, light, motion, pacing, and a clear point of view.
2. **Authorship:** What Russell imagined, designed, programmed, animated, or built.
3. **Depth:** The important technical constraint and the decision that addressed it.
4. **Delivery:** What shipped, was shown, or remains a prototype.
5. **Collaboration:** Who else contributed and how the work fit the larger production.

An employer logo can establish context, but it cannot explain the work.
A technical diagram can explain a mechanism, but footage must carry the experience.

## Success criteria

A new visitor should quickly identify Russell's name, creative direction, a relevant project, and a way to contact him.
A project page should be useful to both a visually led commissioner and an engineer assessing technical depth.
The site should provide direct links that Russell can send for installation, motion capture, or graphics/tooling opportunities.

After launch, assess the relevance of inquiries and which project pages help start conversations.
Do not imply that traffic, a logo strip, or a particular job title guarantees legitimacy or commissions.
