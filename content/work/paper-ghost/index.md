## Software in a physical scene

At Paper Ghost, I developed interactive media for film and television: software, screen visuals, and animation that had to work within a real production.
The work involved collaborating with directors, producers, VFX supervisors, and set crews under tight deadlines.

The challenge was both visual and practical.
An interface had to support the scene, read well on camera, and provide the behavior needed by a performer or operator.

## X-Men: Apocalypse

I created software and digital visuals for the Pentagon video wall and retro computer screens.
The work included maps, timers, clocks, and control displays that helped build the environment seen on camera.

The production was a collaboration.
My responsibilities covered software and visual content, while Zed Axiz supplied hardware and set operators.

## The Glass Castle

I programmed playback software for a rear projection installation used to shoot driving scenes in a studio.
The tools supported 4K/360° video, color calibration, video warping, and wireless playback control from an Android tablet.

This was software designed around the relationship between projected imagery, a physical set, a camera, and the person operating it.

## Interactive props and virtual cameras

For **Quantico**, I developed actor-operated phone and screen applications, including scripted calls, messages, surveillance displays, and timers.
For **Arrival**, I programmed tools to customize military maps and surveillance screen content.

For **mother!**, I programmed a set previsualization application for framing shots and blocking performers.
It included a virtual camera tool using real-world measurements and physically based lens settings, with desktop and Gear VR versions.

## What carries into my practice

These projects taught me to work closely with creative collaborators and to think beyond the application window.
The software needed to fit the space, the equipment, and the story being told.
